# 1. Purpose

This document defines the Compliance Policy for AIOS.

The Compliance Policy establishes the principles, responsibilities, controls, and verification mechanisms required to ensure that AIOS operates in accordance with its internal governance, Shariah requirements, security standards, and applicable legal or regulatory obligations.

Compliance shall be continuously maintained throughout the system lifecycle.

---

# 2. Objectives

The Compliance Policy shall:

* Ensure policy adherence.
* Maintain Shariah compliance.
* Support regulatory readiness.
* Preserve security standards.
* Maintain documentation quality.
* Reduce compliance risk.

---

# 3. Compliance Principles

AIOS compliance shall follow these principles:

* Accountability.
* Transparency.
* Traceability.
* Continuous verification.
* Evidence-based compliance.
* Continuous improvement.

Compliance shall never rely solely on assumptions.

---

# 4. Compliance Domains

Compliance applies to:

* Shariah Compliance.
* Internal Governance.
* Security Policies.
* Documentation Standards.
* Software Development Standards.
* Operational Procedures.
* Data Management.
* Third-Party Integrations.

Every operational domain shall remain compliant.

---

# 5. Shariah Compliance

AIOS shall ensure that:

* Only approved Shariah-compliant securities are eligible for trading.
* Shariah screening data originates from approved providers.
* Compliance status is verified before trade execution.
* Changes in compliance status are reflected in system behavior.
* Trading decisions respect documented Islamic finance rules.

Shariah compliance is mandatory and shall not be bypassed.

---

# 6. Security Compliance

Security compliance shall verify:

* Authentication requirements.
* Authorization controls.
* Credential protection.
* Secure communications.
* Encryption practices.
* Audit logging.

Security policies shall be enforced continuously.

---

# 7. Documentation Compliance

Documentation shall:

* Follow approved templates.
* Maintain version history.
* Remain synchronized with implementation.
* Include approval status.
* Preserve document identifiers.

Documentation quality is part of compliance.

---

# 8. Operational Compliance

Operational compliance shall verify:

* Deployment procedures.
* Backup procedures.
* Disaster recovery readiness.
* Maintenance activities.
* Monitoring processes.

Operational procedures shall follow approved policies.

---

# 9. Third-Party Compliance

External integrations shall satisfy:

* Security requirements.
* Reliability requirements.
* Licensing requirements.
* Compatibility requirements.
* Documented approval.

Approved providers shall be reviewed periodically.

---

# 10. Compliance Verification

Compliance shall be verified through:

* Internal reviews.
* Technical testing.
* Security assessments.
* Documentation reviews.
* Operational audits.

Verification results shall be documented.

---

# 11. Non-Compliance Management

When non-compliance is detected:

* Record the issue.
* Assess the impact.
* Define corrective actions.
* Assign responsibility.
* Verify remediation.
* Close the issue after validation.

Corrective actions shall be fully traceable.

---

# 12. Compliance Reporting

Compliance reports shall include:

* Compliance status.
* Identified issues.
* Corrective actions.
* Outstanding findings.
* Review history.

Reports shall support governance decisions.

---

# 13. Future Expansion

Future compliance capabilities may include:

* Automated compliance validation.
* Continuous policy monitoring.
* AI-assisted compliance reviews.
* Regulatory mapping.
* Policy-as-Code enforcement.

The compliance framework shall evolve with AIOS.

---

# 14. Success Criteria

The Compliance Policy is considered successful when:

* Compliance requirements are consistently satisfied.
* Non-compliance is identified promptly.
* Corrective actions are effective.
* Documentation remains accurate.
* Governance objectives are achieved.
