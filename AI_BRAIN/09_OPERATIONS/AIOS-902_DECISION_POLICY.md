# 1. Purpose

This document defines the official Decision Policy for AIOS.

The Decision Policy establishes the principles, authority levels, approval mechanisms, documentation requirements, and governance processes for all strategic, architectural, technical, operational, and business decisions throughout the AIOS lifecycle.

Every significant decision shall be documented, justified, approved, and traceable.

---

# 2. Objectives

The Decision Policy shall:

* Standardize decision-making.
* Preserve architectural integrity.
* Ensure accountability.
* Improve transparency.
* Reduce inconsistent decisions.
* Maintain historical traceability.

---

# 3. Decision Principles

All decisions shall follow these principles:

* Evidence-based.
* Transparent.
* Documented.
* Reproducible.
* Risk-aware.
* Consistent with project objectives.

Personal preference shall never override documented engineering principles.

---

# 4. Decision Hierarchy

Decisions are categorized into the following hierarchy:

```text
Strategic Decisions

        │

        ▼

Architectural Decisions

        │

        ▼

Technical Decisions

        │

        ▼

Operational Decisions

        │

        ▼

Routine Decisions
```

Higher-level decisions shall take precedence over lower-level decisions.

---

# 5. Decision Categories

AIOS recognizes the following decision categories:

* Strategic Decisions.
* Business Decisions.
* Architecture Decisions.
* Technology Decisions.
* Development Decisions.
* Security Decisions.
* Deployment Decisions.
* Operational Decisions.
* Emergency Decisions.

Each category shall follow the appropriate approval process.

---

# 6. Decision Authority

Decision authority is assigned as follows:

### Strategic Authority

Responsible for:

* Long-term vision.
* Major project direction.
* Investment priorities.

---

### Architecture Authority

Responsible for:

* System architecture.
* Technology stack.
* Design principles.
* Long-term maintainability.

---

### Technical Authority

Responsible for:

* Implementation details.
* Engineering practices.
* Framework selection.
* Technical standards.

---

### Operational Authority

Responsible for:

* Deployment.
* Monitoring.
* Incident response.
* Operational procedures.

---

### Compliance Authority

Responsible for:

* Shariah compliance.
* Security compliance.
* Regulatory compliance.
* Internal policy enforcement.

---

# 7. Architectural Decision Records (ADR)

Major architectural decisions shall be documented as ADRs.

Each ADR shall contain:

* Decision identifier.
* Decision date.
* Context.
* Problem statement.
* Alternatives considered.
* Final decision.
* Rationale.
* Consequences.
* Related documents.

ADRs shall remain permanently archived.

---

# 8. Decision Evaluation

Before approval, each significant decision shall evaluate:

* Technical feasibility.
* Operational impact.
* Security implications.
* Performance implications.
* Scalability.
* Maintainability.
* Compliance requirements.
* Long-term sustainability.

---

# 9. Approval Workflow

Every major decision shall follow:

```text
Proposal

     │

     ▼

Analysis

     │

     ▼

Review

     │

     ▼

Risk Assessment

     │

     ▼

Approval

     │

     ▼

Implementation

     │

     ▼

Documentation
```

No major decision shall bypass governance review.

---

# 10. Emergency Decisions

Emergency decisions may bypass normal approval when:

* Production services are unavailable.
* Security incidents require immediate action.
* Critical data is at risk.

Emergency decisions shall undergo retrospective governance review after implementation.

---

# 11. Decision Documentation

Decision records shall include:

* Unique identifier.
* Decision owner.
* Approval authority.
* Date.
* Supporting evidence.
* Related ADRs.
* Related policies.
* Implementation status.

Documentation shall remain version controlled.

---

# 12. Decision Review

Major decisions shall be reviewed periodically to verify:

* Continued relevance.
* Architectural consistency.
* Operational effectiveness.
* Security compliance.
* Business alignment.

Outdated decisions shall be revised or retired.

---

# 13. Future Expansion

Future Decision Policy capabilities may include:

* AI-assisted decision analysis.
* Automated impact assessment.
* Governance dashboards.
* Decision knowledge graphs.
* Predictive risk analysis.

The decision framework shall evolve alongside AIOS.

---

# 14. Success Criteria

The Decision Policy is considered successful when:

* Decisions are fully traceable.
* Responsibilities are clearly defined.
* Architectural integrity is preserved.
* Risks are evaluated before implementation.
* Decision quality improves over time.
