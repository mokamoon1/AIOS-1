# 1. Purpose

This document defines the Document Control framework for AIOS.

The Document Control framework establishes the policies, standards, responsibilities, versioning rules, review cycles, and approval procedures required to ensure that every AIOS document remains accurate, traceable, consistent, and properly maintained throughout its lifecycle.

Documentation is considered a controlled project asset.

---

# 2. Objectives

The Document Control framework shall:

* Standardize documentation.
* Preserve document integrity.
* Maintain version history.
* Ensure traceability.
* Support governance.
* Enable controlled evolution.

---

# 3. Document Control Principles

Every controlled document shall be:

* Identifiable.
* Versioned.
* Reviewed.
* Approved.
* Traceable.
* Archived.

Uncontrolled documentation shall not be considered authoritative.

---

# 4. Document Lifecycle

Every controlled document shall follow:

```text id="g4zt7v"
Draft

   │

   ▼

Review

   │

   ▼

Approval

   │

   ▼

Publication

   │

   ▼

Maintenance

   │

   ▼

Revision

   │

   ▼

Archive
```

Each lifecycle stage shall be documented.

---

# 5. Document Categories

AIOS documentation includes:

* Project Documentation.
* Architecture Documentation.
* Requirements Documentation.
* Design Documentation.
* Development Documentation.
* Testing Documentation.
* Deployment Documentation.
* Governance Documentation.
* Appendix Documentation.

Each category follows the same governance framework.

---

# 6. Document Identification

Every controlled document shall include:

* Document ID.
* Title.
* Version.
* Status.
* Category.

Document identifiers shall remain unique throughout the project.

---

# 7. Version Control

Version numbers shall follow:

```text id="d2pw8q"
MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH
```

Version increments shall reflect:

* Major architectural revisions.
* Functional enhancements.
* Editorial corrections.

Every revision shall be recorded.

---

# 8. Review Process

Document reviews shall verify:

* Technical accuracy.
* Architectural consistency.
* Formatting compliance.
* Terminology consistency.
* Reference integrity.
* Version correctness.

Review findings shall be documented.

---

# 9. Approval Process

Before publication, each document shall receive:

* Technical review.
* Governance review (when applicable).
* Final approval.

Approved documents become official project references.

---

# 10. Change Tracking

Every document revision shall record:

* Revision date.
* Revision identifier.
* Description of changes.
* Reviewer.
* Approval status.

Historical revisions shall remain recoverable.

---

# 11. Archiving

Archived documents shall:

* Remain immutable.
* Preserve historical value.
* Maintain traceability.
* Remain accessible for audits.

Archived documents shall never replace active versions.

---

# 12. Periodic Review

Controlled documents shall be reviewed periodically to verify:

* Continued relevance.
* Technical accuracy.
* Compliance.
* Architecture alignment.
* Operational applicability.

Outdated documents shall be revised or retired.

---

# 13. Future Expansion

Future Document Control capabilities may include:

* Automated document validation.
* AI-assisted documentation review.
* Cross-reference verification.
* Knowledge graph integration.
* Policy-aware document management.

The documentation framework shall evolve with AIOS.

---

# 14. Success Criteria

The Document Control framework is considered successful when:

* Every document is traceable.
* Version history remains complete.
* Documentation remains synchronized with implementation.
* Governance requirements are satisfied.
* Project knowledge remains preserved.
