# 1. Purpose

This document defines how AIOS identifies, measures, and controls investment risks.

The primary objective is capital protection.

---

# 2. Risk Philosophy

AIOS follows the principle:

"Risk management comes before profit optimization."

No opportunity is acceptable without understanding its risk.

---

# 3. Risk Management Position

Risk evaluation occurs before any portfolio decision.

```text id="1m5g3s"
Investment Opportunity

        ↓

Risk Analysis

        ↓

Portfolio Evaluation

        ↓

Decision
```

---

# 4. Risk Categories

AIOS evaluates multiple risk types.

---

# 4.1 Market Risk

Risk caused by overall market movement.

Examples:

* Market decline.
* Economic uncertainty.
* High volatility.

---

# 4.2 Company Risk

Risk related to the individual company.

Examples:

* Poor financial performance.
* Business problems.
* Competitive pressure.

---

# 4.3 Liquidity Risk

Risk that an asset cannot be traded easily.

Evaluates:

* Trading volume.
* Market activity.

---

# 4.4 Portfolio Risk

Risk caused by portfolio structure.

Examples:

* Excessive concentration.
* Sector imbalance.
* High correlation.

---

# 5. Risk Evaluation Framework

AIOS evaluates:

```text id="pj1y4n"
Opportunity Quality

        +

Market Condition

        +

Volatility

        +

Portfolio Impact

        =

Risk Assessment
```

---

# 6. Position Sizing

AIOS determines investment size based on:

* Portfolio value.
* Risk level.
* Confidence score.
* Maximum exposure limits.

---

# 7. Risk Controls

The system must support:

## Maximum Position Size

Prevents excessive allocation to one security.

---

## Sector Exposure Limit

Controls sector concentration.

---

## Loss Protection

Defines conditions for reducing exposure.

---

## Risk Score

Every opportunity receives a risk evaluation.

---

# 8. Risk Decision Output

Risk Agent produces:

```text id="8u9f7q"
Risk Score

Risk Category

Maximum Allocation

Warnings

Approval Status
```

---

# 9. Trade Approval Rules

A trade cannot proceed if:

* Shariah status is invalid.
* Risk exceeds limits.
* Data quality is insufficient.
* Portfolio exposure becomes unsafe.

---

# 10. Risk Agent Authority

Risk Agent has authority to:

* Reject unsafe opportunities.
* Reduce allocation.
* Request additional analysis.

Risk Agent cannot:

* Create investment decisions alone.
* Change system rules.

---

# 11. Risk Monitoring

AIOS continuously monitors:

* Portfolio exposure.
* Market volatility.
* Position changes.
* Performance.

---

# 12. Risk Reporting

The system must explain:

* Why risk was accepted.
* Why risk was rejected.
* What controls were applied.

---

# 13. Future Expansion

Possible additions:

* Stress testing.
